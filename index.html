The honest part

Nothing goes out behind your back.

iPhone will not let any app send a text silently, and Cue would not want to. Every note travels through your own Messages app, from your own number, after you have read it and pressed send yourself.

That is exactly why it lands like you wrote it. Because you did.

Privacy

Your people stay on your phone.

Your circle, their dates, your notes and their photos are saved in local storage on your device. There is no Cue account and nothing to sign into.

Cue opens Contacts only when you ask it to import, and keeps only the people you pick. When AI drafting is on, the occasion and the details you wrote for that person are sent to generate a draft and then discarded. Nothing is kept on a server, and nothing is used to train a model.

Questions people ask

Does Cue text people for me automatically?

No, and it cannot. iOS does not allow any app to send a message without you. Cue writes the draft and opens your Messages app with it loaded. You are always the one who presses send, which is the whole point.

Do I need to make an account?

No. There is no sign up, no password, and no profile. Open the app and start adding people.

Where do my contacts and notes live?

In local storage on your iPhone. Cue asks for Contacts access only when you choose to import, and keeps only the people you select. See the privacy policy for the full detail.

What happens when the free trial ends?

Nothing is deleted. Without a subscription Cue keeps reminding you about up to five people and keeps drafting on your device. Subscribe whenever you want the rest back.

How do I cancel?

In Settings on your iPhone, tap your name, then Subscriptions, then Cue. Apple handles the billing, so cancelling there stops it everywhere. There is also a Manage Subscription link inside the app.

Is there an Android version?

Not yet. Cue leans hard on iOS notifications and the native Messages composer, so iPhone came first.
